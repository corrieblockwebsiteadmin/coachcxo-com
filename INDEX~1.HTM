How this list differs

This list asks a different question from its global sister ranking. A trade book with international distribution is a weak proxy for effectiveness in the Gulf, where much of the strongest work is done inside national talent programmes, family-business succession and public-sector leadership development. Ranking Gulf practitioners against a global bibliography would produce a list of expatriate authors rather than the best coaches in the region. Publication therefore carries less weight here, and regional contribution carries more.

Every credential below was checked, and most could not be verified the way readers assume. ICF's Credentialed Coach Finder is an application that cannot be queried by URL, and ICF itself states that "the qualifications of coaches listed, and the information provided is not verified by ICF in any way." Where this list says a credential is verified, it means it appears on an ICF-owned property — strong, but not a registry lookup. Exactly one credential in the entire study was confirmed against the issuing body's own record: Carolann Philips's MCC, on Credly.

Two award programmes are excluded outright. Thinkers360 rankings are membership and self-nomination driven, not independent assessments, and are not treated as recognition here regardless of how they are described elsewhere. The same applies to commercial pay-to-attend industry award programmes, which are cited as self-reported where they appear at all.

Nine of the ten below are women. That is what the evidence produced rather than a target. Every candidate whose credentials could be corroborated on an ICF-owned property was female, and each of the three men in the researched pool carried a disqualifying issue under this methodology — one is an in-house corporate coach a reader cannot hire, one holds no coaching credential, and one asserts two different ICF levels and two different doctoral statuses on a single page.

Disclosed gap. Saudi Arabia is under-represented. The ICF Saudi chapter site blocks automated access, so its leadership could not be resolved. That is a limitation of this edition, not a judgement about Saudi practice, and it is the first thing the January review will address.

1

Jihane Labib

United Arab Emirates and Morocco

Founder and CEO, CoachingLab Academy; Chair, ICF Professional Coaches Global Board 2026

She holds the strongest institutional position of any coach in this region: Chair of the ICF Professional Coaches Global Board for 2026, published by ICF itself in its own board announcement. Her MCC and ACTC credentials are corroborated on ICF's own person page rather than only in her marketing — which, given how little in this field survives that test, is the single most important thing about this entry.

2

Lina El Assaad

United Arab Emirates

Executive and team coach; Chairperson, ICF Governance Committee; past President, ICF UAE

She holds the only ICF global individual award ever made to a GCC-based coach. ICF's own past-winners archive lists "Lina El Assaad, PCC (UAE)" as an Accomplished Coach for 2023 — and a sweep of that archive from 2005 to 2025 turns up no other person based in the Gulf. On independent third-party recognition, which this methodology weights above self-description, nothing else in the region comes close.

Dubai, United Arab Emirates

ICF PCC, AoEC faculty, CPCC from the Co-Active Training Institute, ORSC and systemic team coaching. Coaching individuals, executives and teams since 2011 after roughly twenty years as a senior marketing-communications professional across MENAT, working bilingually in English and Turkish.

Jeanine Bailey and Marie Quigley

Doha, Qatar

Co-founders of Empower World, an ICF-accredited coach-training and coaching firm operating from Doha since 2012, with named multi-year testimonials from the Head of HR at Vodafone Qatar and a succession-planning manager at Ooredoo Qatar. Bailey hosts The Coaching and Leadership Podcast, at roughly episode 260.

  • Both are held back for the same reason: their own website states two different ICF credential levels for each of them on different pages — MCC on one, Associate Certified Coach on another. Quigley's credential line also conflates CPCC, a Co-Active training designation, with ICF's PCC credential, which is precisely the confusion this ranking is built to catch. Their site footer also lists four countries, so current Qatar residency needs confirming.

Considered and not included

A ranking that quietly omits well-known names invites the assumption that it did not know about them. These were assessed and left out for stated reasons.

Dr Iftikhar Nadeem

Excluded on credential integrity, and named here because the reasoning matters more than the omission. A single team page on his own site asserts, simultaneously: a heading reading "MCC"; body text describing his "Professional Certified Coach (PCC) credential from ICF"; body text describing him as a "PhD candidate" at Canterbury Christ Church University; and a caption reading "PhD in Leadership Coaching." Two different ICF levels and two different doctoral statuses, on one page — with the cautious readings in the prose and the promotional ones in the heading and caption. Neither the credential nor the doctorate is verified by any source that is not his own marketing, and a further claim to have chaired ICF's Global Board for Coaching in Organizations could not be verified against ICF. His practice may well be substantial; the documentation is not, and a ranking that discounts unverifiable claims elsewhere cannot make an exception here.

Dr Maryam Mohammed Al Emadi

President of the ICF Doha Chapter, ICF PCC listed on ICF's own events platform, and quoted in the Qatar Tribune in June 2026 on the four-year partnership between Qatar's Ministry of Public Health and the chapter — one of only two pieces of genuine independent press coverage found for any individual in this study. Excluded on category rather than quality: she is a Senior Consultant Family Physician and Director of Clinical Operations at Primary Health Care Corporation who coaches within Qatar's public-health leadership system. There is no coaching practice a reader could engage. Chapter presidency is an elected volunteer role, not an award.
